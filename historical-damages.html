Damages.

The Desert locust is the most dangerous migratory pest in the world. A single square kilometre of swarm can contain up to 80 million locusts. Large swarms can have devastating impacts on agriculture, food security, and the livelihoods of millions across Africa, the Middle East, and Asia.

2019-2021 Locust Plague

The 2019-2021 desert locust plague that swept through the horn of Africa is a good example of the damage desert locusts can cause to nations. The horn of Africa is home to millions of people dependent on agriculture and livestock. The massive swarms destroyed crops and pasture across Ethiopia, Kenya, Somalia, and other East African countries, leading to widespread famine and humanitarian crisis affecting millions.

Other Outbreaks

The 2019-2021 desert locust outbreak is just the most recent. Desert locust swarms occur roughly every 2 to 5 years but only a few are truly devastating and make headlines. Historical records show major outbreaks in 1987-1989, 2003-2005, and numerous smaller incidents throughout the 20th century. Each outbreak brings significant economic losses and food security threats to vulnerable populations.
