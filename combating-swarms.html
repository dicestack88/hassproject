Response and prevention

Combating
Swarms.

Various strategies and approaches have been developed to combat desert locust swarms. Through early detection, coordination between nations, and a combination of biological and chemical control methods, we can significantly reduce the devastating impact of these swarms on agriculture and food security.

Sustainable Alternatives for Desert Locust Control

Recent research has recognised the issues prevalent in dangerous pesticide use through environmental damage and future uncertainty about access to pesticide use due to potential European Union trade restrictions on EU banned pesticides. This research has proposed alternative measures to combat out of control desert locust swarms. One of the proposed alternatives is the usage of the aforementioned locust specific pesticide called M. acridum which was used in Somalia. Although there was not much reliable research done on the after effects of such widespread use of M. acridum in Somalia there has been no concerns raised as with other nations.

The other major alternative proposed was the usage of predatory birds to contain locust populations to avoid buildups of locusts that could trigger their gregarious phase and start plagues.

As it stands the optimal strategy to combat desert locust populations is the implementation of a preventive predatory bird strategy to avoid and decrease the chances of a locust plague ever forming.

Chemical and Biological Control

During the 2019-2021 Horn of Africa desert locust swarms, countries used tracking methods to predict locust movements and utilised pesticides to kill locusts. The effectiveness of these methods varies significantly depending on the pesticide used and environmental conditions.

Amidst this dangerous chemical usage Somalia stands as the exception. During the locust swarms Somalia utilized the biopesticide M. acridum which targets locusts specifically and has shown promising results with minimal environmental impact compared to broad-spectrum pesticides.

Key Stakeholders

🏛️

National Governments

Implement national locust monitoring programs, enforce border controls, and coordinate with neighboring countries on early warning systems and response strategies.

🌾

Regional Producers

Farmers and agricultural organizations report locust sightings, adopt preventive farming practices, and participate in community-based pest management initiatives.

🌍

International Organisations

The FAO leads the Desert Locust Information Service, providing coordination, technical expertise, and financial support to affected nations for monitoring and control operations.
