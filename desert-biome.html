Place and habitat

The Desert Biome
& where they live.

Desert Locusts live in the arid/semi-arid deserts, dry grasslands, and shrublands of North Africa, East Africa, the Horn of Africa, the Middle East, and Southern Asia.

The Recession phase

When rainfall is low, locusts remain in their solitary recession phase. They live in clumps of desert trees, shrubs, or grasses because their bodies are perfectly adapted to utilise any type of shade.

  • Night: Desert Locusts spend most of their time high up on trees or tall shrubs to prevent getting eaten by predators
  • Morning: Desert Locusts move down to the ground to bask and warm up in the morning sun.
  • Afternoon: In the Afternoon, most locusts spend time in the shade underneath shrubs to prevent overheating in the scorching desert heat.

The Gregarious phase

Heavy rainfall completely changes the behaviour of desert locusts. Increased moisture triggers rapid vegetation growth, causing locusts to crowd together and transition into their gregarious (swarming) phase. During this phase, they fly astonishing distances during the day, stripping the land clear of crops. At night, massive swarms settle together on nearby vegetation to roost.

Concentration & Breeding Zones

Since Desert Locusts are constantly migrating across the continent, it is extremely hard to map static concentration zones. However, 2 vague areas have more locusts compared to other places. This includes:

  • Morocco and the Western Sahara: These countries act as major core corridors where immature Desert Locusts migrate to.
  • The Red Sea Coastline: This area serves as a massive, high-yield breeding ground because the warm coastal temperatures and moisture perfectly favour reproduction.
